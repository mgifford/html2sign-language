HTML to Sign Language — review status

A research prototype, not a finished translator. This page separates what exists, what is missing, and what a reviewer can actually review today.

There is no sentence-to-sign or page-to-sign feature yet. The public demo has 24 isolated ASL word clips. The research bench produces English-to-gloss lexical plans for 16 prose fixtures; those plans are not ASL and are not played by the avatar.

What exists

24 isolated ASL word clips

The main page can play 24 short clips derived from WLASL. They are single signs with no sentence context. The metadata file has 316 entries, but most do not have an animation file; only the 24 word clips plus idle and test assets resolve.

Open the word demo

16 prose lexical plans

The P2 research bench has precomputed English-to-gloss plans for 16 single-block prose fixtures. A lexical plan shows candidate gloss order and source spans. It has no nonmanual markers, spatial grammar, classifiers, role shift, or avatar playback.

Open the P2 viewer

What is missing

  • Typing a sentence and seeing that sentence signed.
  • Pointing at an HTML page and having it read in sign language.
  • Continuous ASL grammar, nonmanual markers, spatial agreement, classifiers, and role shift.
  • Human-reviewed translations and a licensed production signing set.
  • Any public claim that the avatar is equivalent to a human interpreter.

Why adding words is not enough

Vocabulary is not grammar. Playing one sign after another does not produce an ASL sentence. ASL uses spatial placement, verb direction, facial and head markers, classifiers, role shift, and context-dependent sign choice. A larger dictionary would still not solve those problems.

The research path is therefore: lexical plans first, then a reviewed signing path with licensed or human-translated units. The production route remains human translation, not free-English generation.

What can be reviewed now

  1. Word clips (Deaf ASL reviewer): are these 24 isolated signs recognisable and appropriate? This is a clip-by-clip review, not a sentence translation.
  2. P2 lexical plans (owner/alignment reviewer): do the English source spans, block mapping, omissions, and gloss order match the source? This is not an ASL review.
  3. P2 linguistic plausibility (independent Deaf ASL reviewer): are the lexical sequences plausible for the source, and what is missing? This must be done by a fluent Deaf ASL reviewer, not the owner or implementer.

If you cannot read ASL, do not record an independent ASL review. The review CLI requires an explicit independent-reviewer attestation and the actual reviewer's name; the owner cannot self-attest.

Start with the one-page review table, the review packet, and the P2 viewer.

Roadmap

  1. P2.4: owner alignment review and independent Deaf ASL review of the 16 prose lexical plans.
  2. P3/P4: prose-only browser-AI research (simplification, direct, hybrid); no avatar and no ASL claim.
  3. P5: licensed 3D-LEX or human-translated signing units with Deaf review.
  4. P6: optional playback only after licensing, review, and explicit approval.
